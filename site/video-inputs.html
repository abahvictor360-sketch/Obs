Every source is added the same way: in the Sources dock tap +, pick the type, give it a name, set it up in its Properties (you see a live preview there) and tap Add. Long-press it in the Sources dock or use the bar under the canvas for Properties and Filters later.

Tablet cameras

Any tabletFront or back camera
  1. Sources + › Video Capture Device.
  2. Pick Front or Back, and a Resolution: 720p is recommended; 1080p and 4K need a recent tablet.
  3. Use the camera controls for zoom, torch, exposure and focus lock (stops the camera hunting for focus when people move).
  4. Turn on Mirror front camera if you want the selfie view.

Most tablets can run only one camera at a time. Using the front and back camera in visible scenes together, or a built-in camera with a USB camera, may show an error on one of them. Cameras pause while you’re in another app.

HDMI cameras and capture cards

HDMI capture card (UVC) or USB webcamUSB-C hub or OTG adapteriPad: iPadOS 17 or newer
  1. Connect the camera’s HDMI out to a UVC capture card (most cheap “HDMI to USB” sticks are UVC), and the card to the tablet with a USB-C adapter or hub.
  2. Sources + › USB Video Capture. Android asks for permission the first time: allow it.
  3. Pick the device if more than one is plugged in. The picture appears in the preview.
  4. Sound from the card shows up as a USB sound card: add an Audio Input Capture and choose it, or leave it on Automatic.

Turn off the camera’s on-screen display (HDMI info) in its menu. Use the camera’s “clean HDMI” output at 1080p30 or 720p30. Power the hub: capture cards need more current than the tablet’s port gives.

One USB video device works at a time. If nothing appears: unplug and replug the card, check the tablet supports USB OTG, and try another cable or hub.

Screen Capture: other apps and games

Android 10+ or iPad
  1. Sources + › Screen Capture, then tap Start screen capture.
  2. Android: allow the screen-recording prompt. iPad: choose OBSpad Screen in the broadcast sheet and tap Start Broadcast.
  3. Switch to the app or game. Your other sources stay on top of it and the stream keeps running.
  4. For the app’s sound on Android, add Audio Output Capture.

While OBSpad is open, the screen source shows a status card instead of a mirror of OBSpad itself. Phone-call audio from Zoom, Teams or Meet can’t be captured on Android (the system blocks it): see meetings.

A phone camera with sound (RTMP)

A phone with Larix Broadcaster (free)Same Wi-Fi or network
  1. In OBSpad: Sources + › Phone / Encoder (RTMP / SRT). Keep RTMP selected. The properties show the Server (this tablet’s address), a Stream key and a QR code.
  2. On the phone, install Larix Broadcaster (Android or iPhone).
  3. Scan the QR code with the phone’s normal camera: Larix opens and adds the connection. Or in Larix: Settings › Connections › New, and paste the Full address.
  4. In Larix’s video settings choose H.264 (not HEVC), 1080p or 720p, 30 fps, 4–6 Mbps; audio AAC.
  5. Tap the record button in Larix. Within a second or two OBSpad shows “Receiving”, the picture, and the phone’s sound in the Audio Mixer.

Other apps and encoders

  • Prism Live, Streamlabs mobile, CameraFi Live: Custom RTMP with the Server and Stream key.
  • OBS on a laptop: Settings › Stream › Custom, Server and Stream key from OBSpad.
  • GoPro, DJI, ATEM Mini, vMix, hardware encoders: RTMP URL = Full address (or Server + key).
  • Another OBSpad: Settings › Stream › Custom RTMP with this tablet’s address and key.

Several phones: add one Phone / Encoder source per phone; each needs its own Stream key. If the sound arrives before the picture, use the mixer’s Audio sync offset (timer button).

SRT: phones over weak Wi-Fi or the internet

Larix, OBS, vMix, a hardware encoder or ffmpeg that sends SRT

SRT sends lost pieces again, so the picture stays clean on busy Wi-Fi, mobile data or across the internet. Use it instead of RTMP when RTMP stutters.

  1. Add a Phone / Encoder source and choose SRT in its properties. You get an SRT address like srt://192.168.1.20:9000?streamid=phone123, the host and port, and the Stream ID.
  2. Larix: scan the QR code, or New connection with the SRT address; mode Caller.
  3. OBS: Settings › Stream › Custom, Server = the SRT address, Stream key empty.
  4. vMix, Haivision, Teradek, Magewell and other encoders: SRT caller mode, the tablet’s IP and port 9000, Stream ID = the key, latency 120–500 ms.
  5. Leave the passphrase / encryption off; encrypted callers are refused. Use H.264 video and AAC audio.

Over the internet the phone must reach the tablet: forward UDP port 9000 on the router to the tablet, or use a VPN like Tailscale or ZeroTier on both devices and use the tablet’s VPN address.

Old phones as webcams and IP cameras

DroidCam or IP Webcam on the phone, or an IP camera
  1. Sources + › Network Video (phone / IP camera), then choose the Source:
    • DroidCam (Android/iPhone): open the app, enter the WiFi IP it shows. Switch front/back in the DroidCam app.
    • IP Webcam (Android): tap Start server, enter its IP. Pick Back or Front camera right in OBSpad.
    • MJPEG URL: IP cameras and ESP32-CAMs, e.g. http://user:password@camera.local/mjpg/video.mjpg.
    • Stream URL: HLS (.m3u8) or an HTTP video stream.

These carry video only and are lighter on quality. For a phone camera with sound, use Phone / Encoder instead.

NDI® sources

NDI runtime in the buildSame network (wired or good Wi-Fi)
  1. Sources + › NDI® Source. OBSpad searches the network and lists the senders, e.g. STUDIO-PC (vMix - Output 1).
  2. Pick one. Video and sound arrive together; the sound gets a fader in the Audio Mixer.
  3. On busy Wi-Fi turn on Low bandwidth (NDI’s preview stream).

Senders: vMix, OBS with DistroAV, NDI PTZ cameras, the NDI HX Camera app on a phone, Microsoft Teams with NDI turned on, Zoom Rooms, or another OBSpad with NDI output.

NDI needs the NDI® runtime, which is included only when the build is made with the NDI licence accepted (see ndi.video). Otherwise the source explains it isn’t available.

Guest cameras by link (no app)

The guest needs a phone or laptop with a browserInternet on both sides
  1. Sources + › Guest Camera (link). The properties show a QR code and the Guest link.
  2. Send the link (WhatsApp, email) or let the guest scan the QR code. They allow the camera and tap Start.
  3. Their picture appears a few seconds later. New link makes a fresh one; the old one stops working.

The guest’s sound isn’t included (a web page’s sound can’t be mixed into the stream). For a remote guest with sound, ask them to stream with Larix to SRT, or use a Zoom/Teams recipe below.

Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet

Meeting apps don’t let other apps take their video directly, and Android blocks recording call sound. These recipes work today with OBSpad’s inputs:

MeetingBest way into OBSpadWhat you need
ZoomCustom live stream to a Phone / Encoder (RTMP) sourcePaid Zoom plan; “Allow live streaming › Custom live streaming service” on in Zoom settings; the host starts it from Zoom on a computer.
Microsoft TeamsNDI (clean feed per person) into an NDI Source, or RTMP-Out (“Custom Streaming” app) into a Phone / Encoder (RTMP) sourceAdmin turns on NDI or live streaming in the meeting policy; Teams on a Windows or Mac computer on the same network.
Google MeetA laptop in the meeting, its HDMI out into a capture card; or OBS on the laptop sending to SRTMeet only live-streams to YouTube, so a laptop is the bridge.
Any meetingThe universal fallback: laptop HDMI out › capture card › USB Video Capture, its sound through the cardA laptop, HDMI capture card, hub

Zoom, step by step

  1. In OBSpad add a Phone / Encoder (RTMP) source and note the Server and Stream key.
  2. Zoom web settings › In Meeting (Advanced) › turn on Allow live streaming meetings › Custom Live Streaming Service.
  3. In the meeting (Zoom on a computer): More › Live on Custom Live Streaming Service. Stream URL = the Server, Stream key = the key, Live streaming page URL = any page (e.g. your church site).
  4. Spotlight the speaker so they fill the picture. Zoom sends the meeting view and its sound, a few seconds behind.

Teams with NDI, step by step

  1. The Teams admin enables NDI in the meeting policy (“Allow NDI streaming”).
  2. In Teams on the computer: Settings › App permissions › turn on NDI. In the meeting: More › Video effects / Streaming › Broadcast over NDI.
  3. In OBSpad add an NDI Source; each person appears as their own source. Pick the speaker.

For a single remote speaker, the simplest and lowest-delay option is often to skip the meeting app: send them a Guest Camera link, or ask them to stream with Larix to SRT (with sound).

Videos, pictures and slides

Files on the tablet, a USB drive, Google Drive, Dropbox or a link
  1. Media Source for videos: Browse files…, Photos / gallery…, or From a link… (Google Drive and Dropbox share links, or a direct link to a file). Files are copied into OBSpad so they keep working offline.
  2. Choose Loop, and under Audio Monitoring whether you hear it on the tablet. Play, pause and restart from the bar under the canvas.
  3. Image for pictures and lower thirds; Image Slide Show for song lyrics, announcements and photo loops with a transition and timing.

YouTube and Facebook videos can’t be downloaded in OBSpad: download your own video on the site first. Videos in H.264 MP4 play best.

Web pages and overlays

A link or a local HTML file
  1. Sources + › Browser, enter the URL (or a local HTML file), and the page size (e.g. 1920×1080 for a full-screen overlay).
  2. Use it for scoreboards, chat boxes, alerts, countdowns and lower-third widgets. Transparent pages show the scene behind them.
  3. Install overlay packs from GitHub or a .zip in Tools › Plugins; they appear under Add Source › From plugins.

Make it look right

  • Position and size: drag the source on the canvas; pinch to scale, twist to rotate; or Edit › Transform for Fit to screen, Center and Stretch.
  • Crop: in Properties › Transform; hold a side to crop black bars from a capture card.
  • Filters: Chroma Key for green screens, Color Correction, Apply LUT for a film look, masks for round webcams.
  • Sound in sync: the timer button in the Audio Mixer delays a mic or video’s sound up to 2 seconds. Clap on camera and adjust until it matches.
  • Preview first: in Studio Mode, build the next shot in Preview and send it with Transition, a quick transition or the T-bar.

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
Phone / Encoder says “Waiting”Both devices on the same network; the address is the tablet’s (shown in properties); the source is on Program or Preview; stream key matches exactly; the phone isn’t on mobile data.
“Set video to H.264”The phone or encoder is sending HEVC/H.265: switch its codec to H.264 (AVC). Sound must be AAC.
SRT refuses to connectTurn off the passphrase; check the Stream ID; port 9000 UDP must be reachable (router, firewall, VPN).
Picture stutters over Wi-FiLower the phone’s bitrate (3–4 Mbps), use 5 GHz Wi-Fi, switch to SRT, or use a USB-C Ethernet adapter on the tablet.
Camera error when adding a second cameraMost tablets run one camera at a time: use a phone (Phone / Encoder) for the second angle.
Capture card not foundPowered hub, another cable, replug; iPad needs iPadOS 17.
NDI source not listedSame subnet, multicast allowed on the router, tap the refresh button; check the build includes NDI.
Sound early or lateAudio sync offset in the mixer.
